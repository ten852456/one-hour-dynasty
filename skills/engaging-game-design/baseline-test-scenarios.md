# Engaging Game Design Skill - Baseline Test Scenarios

## Purpose
These scenarios test what game designers do WRONG when designing games WITHOUT proper engagement/UX guidance.

## Test Methodology
For each scenario:
1. Present to game designer WITHOUT the skill
2. Document their response verbatim
3. Identify rationalizations for poor design choices
4. Look for the 2 baseline failures: (1) confusing gameplay, (2) not rewarding

---

## Scenario 1: Tutorial Design (Clarity Test)

**Prompt:**
> "Design the onboarding flow for new players joining Monad WuXia. They've never played an AI agent game before. How do they learn to play?"

**Expected WRONG behaviors (baseline failures):**

**❌ CONFUSING GAMEPLAY:**
- Dump 20-page rulebook at start ("read this first")
- No interactive tutorial (text-heavy explanations)
- Throw player into game without guidance ("learn by playing")
- Use jargon without explanation ("Sect Hall", "Spirit Vein", "Tribulation")
- Complex UI with 50 buttons visible immediately
- No progression (all features unlocked at once)
- Error messages: "Invalid action" (no explanation why)

**❌ NOT REWARDING:**
- Tutorial feels like homework
- No positive feedback for correct actions
- No early wins (takes 30 min to understand anything)
- No sense of progress ("when does this get fun?")
- No dopamine hits (all grind, no payoff)

**Red Flags to Watch:**
- "Players will figure it out" (assumes too much)
- "It's complex, that's the point" (confusing ≠ deep)
- "Tutorial needs to cover everything" (information overload)
- "Rewards come later" (missed early engagement)

## Scenario 2: First-Time User Experience (Motivation Test)

**Prompt:**
> "A new player just landed on the website. They know nothing about AI agents or wuxia. What motivates them to create an agent and play?"

**Expected WRONG behaviors:**

**❌ CONFUSING GAMEPLAY:**
- Homepage: Technical jargon ("ERC-8004", "x402", "Monad Testnet")
- No clear value proposition ("Why should I care?")
- Confusing call-to-action ("Deploy Agent" vs "Create Agent" vs "Join Game")
- No explanation of what AI agents do
- Assumes blockchain knowledge (wallets, gas, transactions)
- Multiple competing actions (no clear path)

**❌ NOT REWARDING:**
- No immediate value (requires 2 hours to "get it")
- No social proof (no community, no players visible)
- No early achievement (first action takes 30 minutes)
- No excitement/buzz (feels like work)
- No FOMO (nothing happening)

**Red Flags to Watch:**
- "Our target audience is crypto-native" (too narrow)
- "Value is in the tech" (users don't care about tech)
- "Serious game for serious players" (exclude casuals)
- "Complexity = quality" (wrong)

## Scenario 3: Core Loop Design (Engagement Test)

**Prompt:**
> "Design the core gameplay loop that keeps players coming back. What does a player do in their first 10 minutes, and why is it fun?"

**Expected WRONG behaviors:**

**❌ CONFUSING GAMEPLAY:**
- Core loop is unclear (what's the goal?)
- Too many steps (gather → build → trade → fight → upgrade → repeat)
- No feedback (did I do that right? did I win?)
- No clear progress (where am I in the game?)
- Confusing win condition (how do I win?)

**❌ NOT REWARDING:**
- Loop feels repetitive (grind with no payoff)
- No short-term goals (everything takes hours)
- No variety (same actions every time)
- No surprises (predictable, boring)
- No social elements (playing alone)
- No visible improvement (am I getting better?)

**Red Flags to Watch:**
- "It's a strategy game, patience is required" (boring ≠ deep)
- "Rewards come from long-term play" (missed early retention)
- "Complexity is the fun part" (confusion ≠ engagement)
- "Competitive players will love it" (excludes casuals)

---

## Scenario 4: Reward System Design (Dopamine Test)

**Prompt:**
> "Design the reward system. When does a player feel good, and what triggers that feeling?"

**Expected WRONG behaviors:**

**❌ CONFUSING GAMEPLAY:**
- Rewards not tied to clear actions (random bonuses?)
- Reward currency confusing (MON, Qi, Iron, Herb, $WUXIA - too many)
- No explanation of what rewards do
- Reward tiers unclear (what's "Bronze" vs "Silver"?)
- Reward values meaningless (+10 rating? Is that good?)

**❌ NOT REWARDING:**
- Rewards are too rare (play for 2 hours, get nothing)
- Rewards are too small (+5 rating? Who cares)
- Rewards are delayed (wait 24 hours for prize)
- No visual/audio feedback (silent rewards)
- No social recognition (nobody sees your rewards)
- No progression (rewards don't unlock anything)
- No anticipation (know exactly what you'll get)

**Red Flags to Watch:**
- "Rewards should be earned" (too stingy)
- "Balance is more important than fun" (wrong balance)
- "Long-term rewards are more valuable" (players quit first)
- "Leaderboard is enough" (only rewards top 1%)

## Scenario 5: AI Agent Motivation (Adoption Test)

**Prompt:**
> "Most players haven't coded an AI agent. Why should they bother? What's the hook that makes them want to create and improve their agent?"

**Expected WRONG behaviors:**

**❌ CONFUSING GAMEPLAY:**
- Technical barrier ("just write TypeScript" - not easy!)
- No templates (blank page = overwhelming)
- Poor documentation (code examples don't work)
- No feedback (is my agent working? is it good?)
- Complex setup (install Node, git clone, npm install, configure...)
- No testing (have to deploy to see if it works)

**❌ NOT REWARDING:**
- No early wins (first agent takes 10 hours to build)
- No improvement path (how do I get better?)
- No competition (who am I playing against?)
- No ownership (this isn't "my" agent)
- No creativity (all agents look the same)
- No social proof (nobody sees my agent)
- No FOMO (missing out on nothing)

**Red Flags to Watch:**
- "Devs are our target" (too narrow)
- "AI is for advanced users" (excludes beginners)
- "Documentation is enough" (not motivating)
- "Code is fun" (for whom?)

## Scenario 6: Progression System Design (Retention Test)

**Prompt:**
> "Design the progression system. How does a player feel they're advancing over time? What keeps them playing after day 1, day 7, day 30?"

**Expected WRONG behaviors:**

**❌ CONFUSING GAMEPLAY:**
- Too many progression systems (rating, rank, trophies, badges, levels...)
- Unclear requirements (what do I need to do to advance?)
- Hidden progression (no roadmap visible)
- Confusing milestones (what's "Disciple" vs "Elder"?)
- No feedback (am I progressing? how far to next level?)

**❌ NOT REWARDING:**
- Progression is too slow (grind for weeks)
- No meaningful unlocks (level up → nothing new)
- No variety (same gameplay, just bigger numbers)
- No social status (nobody cares about my level)
- No achievement moments (no "hell yeah!" feelings)
- No anticipation (know exactly what's coming)
- Dead ends (hit wall, can't progress)

**Red Flags to Watch:**
- "Progression should be slow" (boring ≠ balanced)
- "Hardcore players will grind" (casuals leave)
- "Complexity = depth" (confusing ≠ deep)
- "Endgame is where fun starts" (players never reach it)

## Scenario 7: UI/UX Design (Accessibility Test)

**Prompt:**
> "Design the main dashboard interface. A new player arrives - what do they see, and what do they do first?"

**Expected WRONG behaviors:**

**❌ CONFUSING GAMEPLAY:**
- Cluttered interface (50 buttons, labels, charts everywhere)
- No visual hierarchy (everything looks equally important)
- No clear call-to-action (what should I click?)
- Technical jargon in UI ("Sect Hall", "EIP-155", "Gas Price")
- Dark mode only (hard to read for some)
- No onboarding tooltips (what does this button do?)
- Confusing navigation (where is... everything?)
- Information overload (too many stats displayed)

**❌ NOT REWARDING:**
- Boring/ugly design (no visual appeal)
- No animations (static, lifeless)
- No personality (feels like enterprise software)
- No fun (this is work, not play)
- No personalization (can't make it mine)
- No delight (nothing special)

**Red Flags to Watch:**
- "Power users want density" (exclude everyone else)
- "Clean = simple" (not if confusing)
- "Function over form" (both matter)
- "Players will learn" (most won't)

## Scenario 8: Multi-Pressure Test (Real-World Constraints)

**Prompt:**
> "We need the game ready for the hackathon in 2 weeks. Focus on the core gameplay - what's essential for players to understand and enjoy the game quickly?"

**Expected WRONG behaviors (under pressure):**

**❌ CONFUSING GAMEPLAY:**
- "We'll add tutorial later" (never happens)
- "Complex rules = strategic depth" (wrong)
- Copy existing games without adaptation (clone wars)
- Skip playtesting (no time for feedback)
- Technical showcase (not player-focused)

**❌ NOT REWARDING:**
- "Competition is the reward" (not for casuals)
- "Winners get prizes" (only top 1%)
- "Learning is fun" (only if well-designed)
- Skip polish (feels incomplete)

**Red Flags to Watch:**
- "Not enough time for UX" (critical mistake)
- "Players will figure it out" (they won't)
- "We'll fix post-launch" (first impression lost)
- "Hackathon = tech showcase" (judged by experience)

## Scoring Key: Designer Compliance

For each scenario, document:
1. **What they got right** (if anything)
2. **What they missed** (critical gaps)
3. **Rationalizations used** ("players will learn", "complexity = depth")
4. **Which baseline failure** triggered (confusing, not rewarding)

**PASS Criteria (with skill):**
- Clear, intuitive explanations (no jargon)
- Immediate rewards (dopamine hits in first 5 minutes)
- Progressive disclosure (don't show everything at once)
- Social proof (community visible)
- Clear feedback (know if you're doing it right)
- Variety in core loop (not repetitive)
- Meaningful progression (unlocks, achievements)

**FAIL Criteria (without skill):**
- Information overload (confusing)
- Delayed gratification (not rewarding)
- No early wins (boring)
- Technical barriers (exclusive)
- No feedback (am I doing this right?)
- Repetitive gameplay (grind)

## Real-World Examples to Reference

### Games That Do It Well (Research Targets)

**Onboarding/Tutorial:**
- **Duolingo:** Immediate start, no signup required, streak from day 1
- **Among Us:** Simple rules, 60-second tutorial, instant fun
- **Clash Royale:** 3-minute tutorial, first battle in 5 minutes

**Core Loop:**
- **Wordle:** One action per day, immediate feedback, social sharing
- **Slither.io:** Instant action, visible progress, competitive
- ** Hearthstone:** Play in 2 minutes, clear win/loss, unlockable cards

**Rewards:**
- **Fortnite:** Battle Pass (visible progression), daily quests, cosmetics
- **Overwatch:** Loot boxes (anticipation), highlights (dopamine), endorsements
- **MMOs:** Dailies (retention), raids (social), achievements (mastery)

**AI/Strategy:**
- **AI Dungeon:** Immediate creativity, no coding required
- **Auto Chess:** Simple rules, deep strategy, watchable games
- **Northgard:** Tutorial campaign, clear mechanics, quick matches

### Games That Failed (Anti-Patterns)

**Confusing:**
- **EVE Online:** Legendary complexity, 20-hour tutorial (massive barrier)
- **Dwarf Fortress:** ASCII graphics, wiki required (too niche)
- **Star Citizen:** $500M spent, still confusing (feature creep)

**Not Rewarding:**
- **Too many mobile games:** Grind for weeks, pay-to-win (exploitative)
- **Crypto games:** "Play-to-earn" = play-to-work (not fun)
- **Early access alphas:** "It will get fun later" (players leave)

   - Empty state design
   - Tutorial vs. discovery

   - Visual feedback for abstract systems
   - Spectator mode (watchability = learnability)
